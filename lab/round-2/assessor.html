Round 2 · Assessor

Your role: assessor

Your purpose

Understand what the person meant by their concerning statement, whether thoughts of suicide or self-harm are present, what has been happening recently, what has changed, what helps them remain safe, and what they need now.

You do not need to ask every question or cover every area. Listen to the person and decide what is important to follow up.

Before you begin

  • How will you explain what you are trying to understand?
  • How will you begin?
  • What does the person need to understand about why you are asking?
  • Remember that this is a conversation, not a checklist.

A path through the conversation

Move from the person's words toward direct and increasingly specific inquiry. Open each step as you need it.

1. Clarify what you heard

Do not interpret vague language for the person.

Possible ways to open:

  • “When you say ___, what do you mean?”
  • “Have you been thinking about suicide?”
  • “Have you had thoughts about killing yourself?”

Direct language can help you understand what the person is actually experiencing.

2. Understand the thoughts
  • when they began
  • how often they occur
  • how long they last
  • intensity
  • whether they are changing
  • what tends to happen before them
  • what happens when they occur
3. Intent
  • whether the person has wanted to act on the thoughts
  • whether they think they might act
  • whether there has been a moment when they intended to die
  • what stopped, interrupted or changed that moment
4. Method, plan & preparation

Explore whether the person has:

  • thought about how they might die
  • developed a plan
  • chosen a time or place
  • taken preparatory actions
  • rehearsed or researched
  • made arrangements in anticipation of death
5. Access

Understand whether the person currently has access to a method they have considered.

6. Previous behaviour
  • previous suicide attempts
  • aborted or interrupted attempts
  • previous preparation
  • previous self-harm
  • what the person intended when previous self-harm occurred

Do not assume that all self-harm had suicidal intent.

7. Current circumstances
  • physical pain
  • relationship changes
  • work
  • money
  • sleep
  • alcohol or other substances
  • isolation
  • losses
  • other recent changes or pressures
8. What is helping the person stay alive?
  • people
  • responsibilities
  • commitments
  • beliefs or values
  • future events
  • coping
  • ambivalence
  • reasons for living
  • help-seeking
  • what they do during the most difficult moments

Do not reduce this to a generic “protective factors” checklist.

9. What does the person need now?

Return to the person's perspective and immediate needs.

If the person tells you something concerning, stay with the conversation. Ask what you need to understand rather than rushing to reassure, minimize, or solve it.

Your task in this encounter is to understand. You are not being asked to assign a low, moderate, or high risk score.

Before you decide you understand

  • What do I know because the person told me?
  • What am I inferring?
  • What have I not asked?
  • What might another assessment lens reveal?
After the conversation

When the instructor calls time, use these prompts for paired or verbal reflection. Nothing is collected here.

  • What did you learn that felt important?
  • What influenced what you asked next?
  • What do you still not know?
  • Was there something you hesitated or avoided asking?
