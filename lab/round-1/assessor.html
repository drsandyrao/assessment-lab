Round 1 · Assessor

Your role: assessor

Your purpose

Develop a broad understanding of what has been happening in this person's life, what may be contributing to their current concerns, what is supporting them, and what matters to them.

You do not need to ask every question or cover every area. Listen to the person and decide what is important to follow up.

Before you begin

  • How will you explain what you are trying to understand?
  • How will you begin?
  • What does the person need to understand about why you are asking?
  • Remember that this is a conversation, not a checklist.

Start with the person's concern

Before moving across areas of life, try to understand:

  • What brought the person here?
  • Why now?
  • What feels most important to them?
  • What are they hoping will be different?

Areas you might explore

Open an area when it becomes relevant. These are possible directions, not required questions.

Body & health
  • physical health
  • pain or symptoms
  • medications or treatment
  • sleep
  • eating
  • energy
  • sexual or reproductive health when relevant
  • everyday functioning
Thoughts, feelings & coping
  • mood
  • anxiety or stress
  • thoughts and beliefs
  • coping
  • alcohol, cannabis or other substances when relevant
  • previous mental health experiences or supports
  • safety when relevant
Relationships & social life
  • intimate relationships
  • family
  • caregiving
  • friendships
  • social connection
  • conflict
  • support
Everyday conditions
  • work or school
  • housing
  • money
  • transportation
  • responsibilities
  • access to resources
Identity, culture & belonging
  • identities important to the person
  • culture
  • language
  • spirituality, faith or meaning when relevant
  • community
  • belonging or exclusion
Strengths & resources
  • what is going well
  • skills
  • coping resources
  • people and resources they can draw on
  • interests
  • what gives meaning, enjoyment or connection
Goals
  • what the person wants
  • priorities
  • what change would look like

You are not trying to complete every domain. Follow what seems important. Notice what you choose to ask about and what you leave unexplored.

Before you decide you understand

  • What do I know because the person told me?
  • What am I inferring?
  • What have I not asked?
  • What might another assessment lens reveal?
After the conversation

When the instructor calls time, use these prompts for paired or verbal reflection. Nothing is collected here.

  • What did you learn that felt important?
  • What influenced what you asked next?
  • What do you still not know?
  • Was there something you hesitated or avoided asking?
