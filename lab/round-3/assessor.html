Round 3 · Assessor

Your role: assessor

Your purpose

Understand what is happening in the person's relationship and home, whether there are concerns about safety, violence, coercion or control, what has changed, and what the person wants.

You do not need to ask every question or cover every area. Listen to the person and decide what is important to follow up.

Before you begin

  • How will you explain what you are trying to understand?
  • How will you begin?
  • What does the person need to understand about why you are asking?
  • Remember that this is a conversation, not a checklist.

Begin with the relationship and home

Possible openings:

  • What has home been like lately?
  • Has anything changed?
  • What happens when you disagree?
  • Are there things you have changed because of how another person might respond?

Areas for behaviourally specific inquiry

Ask about actual behaviours and their effects rather than relying only on labels.

Control & autonomy
  • decision-making
  • movement
  • communication
  • contact with friends or family
  • appointments
  • money
  • medications
  • transportation
  • privacy

The task is to understand what happens, not merely whether the person uses the word “controlling.”

Fear & intimidation
  • feeling afraid
  • threats
  • intimidation
  • destruction of belongings
  • blocking exits
  • preventing someone from leaving
  • monitoring
Physical behaviour
  • grabbing
  • pushing
  • restraining
  • hitting
  • throwing objects
  • physically preventing movement
  • injuries
Sex & consent
  • whether sexual contact is wanted
  • what happens when the person says no
  • pressure, persistence or guilt
  • whether they feel able to change their mind
  • unwanted sexual contact

Do not assume that being married or partnered establishes consent.

Changes over time
  • Is this new?
  • Has something changed?
  • Is it becoming more frequent?
  • Is it becoming more intense?
  • How is the relationship different from before?
Current safety
  • whether the person feels safe going home
  • what they are worried could happen
  • what happens during escalation
  • whether they have somewhere they could go if they choose
  • who knows what has been happening
  • supports and resources

What does the person want?

Return to the person's goals, choices and definition of what would be helpful.

Assessment does not mean deciding for the person what they should do.

Learning that something happened does not mean you need the whole story. Ask for information that has a purpose in the present assessment.

Before you decide you understand

  • What do I know because the person told me?
  • What am I inferring?
  • What have I not asked?
  • What might another assessment lens reveal?
After the conversation

When the instructor calls time, use these prompts for paired or verbal reflection. Nothing is collected here.

  • What did you learn that felt important?
  • What influenced what you asked next?
  • What do you still not know?
  • Was there something you hesitated or avoided asking?
